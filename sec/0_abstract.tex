\begin{abstract}
Video diffusion models normally run one denoising trajectory for every
requested control value. We introduce \textbf{Latent-Function Video
Diffusion (LFVD)}, which instead samples one family-valued latent object and
queries complete videos from it at arbitrary continuous controls. A learned
family encoder and lightweight query decoder are trained in frozen video-VAE
space; a pretrained Video DiT then learns the family-code distribution with
standard flow matching. On five object-disjoint Lighting folds, LFVD uses one
trajectory for nine queries and attains LPIPS .3216 versus .3630 for a
strengthened shared-noise Pointwise model with explicit control tokens, while
their control correlations are statistically tied (.933 versus .930). A
high-exposure audit shows that Pointwise can approach LFVD's per-query quality
with nine times as many updates, so we do not attribute the matched-update
fidelity gap solely to the representation. Instead, LFVD's distinctive
evidence is functional: its generated query path matches the measured target
path substantially better (adjacent-step LPIPS error .030 versus .087), and
its learned reader improves LPIPS from .407 to .327 over linear interpolation
of the same sampled family code. With three observed Lighting controls, a
nine-times-update Pointwise stress test and LFVD trade LPIPS against PSNR and
ordering at six measured unseen controls. On MEAD, LFVD remains comparable in
fidelity but not superior in ordering. LFVD becomes faster than memory-feasible
batched Pointwise at approximately seven Lighting queries. These results
support family-valued diffusion as an amortized interface for coherent
continuous video queries rather than an unconditional quality advantage.

\end{abstract}
