\begin{abstract}
Video diffusion models support continuous control over video attributes,
such as motion intensity and camera speed.
Conventional models require a separate denoising process for each
control value, even when the resulting videos share much of their
visual content. We refer to this approach as \emph{pointwise generation}.
We introduce \textbf{Latent-Function Video Diffusion (LFVD)}, a framework
that generates a shared latent representation of videos across a
continuous control range using a single diffusion process.
A lightweight query decoder then produces a complete video at any
requested control value without additional denoising.
LFVD learns this representation by encoding videos captured at different
control values and training the query decoder to reconstruct their video
latents. A pretrained video diffusion transformer subsequently learns
to generate the shared representation using standard flow matching.
Experiments on lighting and facial-expression control demonstrate
that LFVD generates multiple videos with competitive visual quality
using a single diffusion process. LFVD improves video quality and
control consistency in lighting tasks, including at unseen control
values, while maintaining comparable video quality in facial-expression
generation. Although control consistency varies across tasks, LFVD
reduces generation time when multiple control values are requested.
These results highlight the potential of shared latent representations
for efficient and continuous video generation.

\end{abstract}
